## The three flavours of learning

Chapter 8's own organising idea — §8.2, §8.3 and §8.4 are three answers to one question.

| | §8.2 empirical risk | §8.3 maximum likelihood / MAP | §8.4 Bayesian inference |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| minimise / maximise | $R_{\text{emp}}$ | $-\log p(\mathbf{y} \mid \boldsymbol\theta)$ | nothing — **integrate** |
| needs a prior | no | MLE no, MAP yes | yes |
| needs $p(\mathcal{X})$ | no | no, it cancels | **yes**, it is an integral |
| computational problem | optimization | optimization | **integration** |
| the answer | a point | a point | a **distribution** |
| far-extrapolation coverage | — | $0.1796$ | $\mathbf{0.9498}$ |

## §8.1 Data, models, and learning

| term | meaning |
| --- | --- |
| **data as vectors** | numerical, with a fixed dimension and an agreed ordering |
| **model as a function** | a predictor $f: \mathbb{R}^D \to \mathbb{R}$ |
| **model as a distribution** | a probabilistic description of the process that made the data |
| **learning** | finding parameters that generalise, not that memorise |

The three phases of learning: **prediction / inference**, **training / parameter estimation**, and
**hyperparameter tuning / model selection**.

## §8.2 Empirical risk minimization

| what | equation | notes |
| --- | --- | --- |
| affine predictor | $f(\mathbf{x}_n, \boldsymbol\theta) = \boldsymbol\theta^\top\mathbf{x}_n$ | Eq 8.2, with the bias folded in |
| the unit-feature trick | $\mathbf{x}_n \leftarrow [1, x_{n,1}, \ldots]^\top$ | Eq 8.4; makes affine look linear |
| **empirical risk** | $R_{\text{emp}}(f, \mathbf{X}, \mathbf{y}) = \frac{1}{N}\sum_{n=1}^{N}\ell(y_n, \hat{y}_n)$ | **Eq 8.7** |
| squared loss | $\ell(y_n, \hat{y}_n) = (y_n - \hat{y}_n)^2$ | Eq 8.6 |
| least-squares risk | $\frac{1}{N}\lVert\mathbf{y} - \mathbf{X}\boldsymbol\theta\rVert^2$ | Eq 8.8 |
| **normal equations** | $\boldsymbol\theta^* = (\mathbf{X}^\top\mathbf{X})^{-1}\mathbf{X}^\top\mathbf{y}$ | Eq 8.9 |
| **expected risk** | $R_{\text{true}}(f) = \mathbb{E}_{(\mathbf{x},y)}[\ell(y, f(\mathbf{x}))]$ | **Eq 8.10** — what you actually want |
| the gap | $R_{\text{emp}}$ is computable, $R_{\text{true}}$ is not | the whole chapter lives here |

### §8.2.3 Regularization

| what | equation | notes |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **regularized risk** | $\min_{\boldsymbol\theta}\ \frac{1}{N}\lVert\mathbf{y} - \mathbf{X}\boldsymbol\theta\rVert^2 + \lambda\lVert\boldsymbol\theta\rVert^2$ | **Eq 8.12** |
| ridge solution | $\boldsymbol\theta = \left(\frac{\mathbf{X}^\top\mathbf{X}}{N} + \lambda\mathbf{I}\right)^{-1}\frac{\mathbf{X}^\top\mathbf{y}}{N}$ | always invertible for $\lambda > 0$ |
| $\ell_1$ version | $\lambda\lVert\boldsymbol\theta\rVert_1$, **lasso** | sets coefficients to **exactly** zero |
| **the reading** | $\lambda$ trades fit against $\lVert\boldsymbol\theta\rVert$ | and equals $\sigma^2/(N\tau^2)$ — see §8.3.2 |

### §8.2.4 Cross-validation

$$
\mathbb{E}_{\mathcal{V}}[R(\mathcal{V} \mid M)] \approx \frac{1}{K}\sum_{k=1}^{K} R(\mathcal{V}^{(k)} \mid M) \qquad \text{(Eq 8.39)}
$$

with **standard error** $\sigma/\sqrt{K}$, $\sigma$ the spread of the per-fold risks.

| $K$ | mean estimate | spread | bias vs truth |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| $2$ | $0.238485$ | $0.146749$ | $+0.045981$ |
| $5$ | $0.193586$ | $0.055525$ | $+0.001082$ |
| $10$ | $0.188288$ | $0.048385$ | $-0.004215$ |
| $40$ | $0.186229$ | $0.045937$ | $-0.006274$ |

Small $K$ trains on less data and **overstates** the risk. $K = 5$ or $10$ captures nearly all the
variance reduction.

## §8.3 Parameter estimation

### §8.3.1 Maximum likelihood

| what | equation | notes |
| --- | --- | --- |
| likelihood | $p(y_n \mid \mathbf{x}_n, \boldsymbol\theta)$ | a function of $\boldsymbol\theta$ with data fixed |
| **negative log-likelihood** | $\mathcal{L}(\boldsymbol\theta) = -\sum_{n=1}^{N}\log p(y_n \mid \mathbf{x}_n, \boldsymbol\theta)$ | **Eq 8.14** |
| Gaussian noise | $p(y_n \mid \mathbf{x}_n, \boldsymbol\theta) = \mathcal{N}(y_n \mid \mathbf{x}_n^\top\boldsymbol\theta, \sigma^2)$ | Eq 8.17 |
| worked out | $\mathcal{L} = \frac{1}{2\sigma^2}\sum_n (y_n - \mathbf{x}_n^\top\boldsymbol\theta)^2 + \text{const}$ | **Eq 8.18d** |

:::tip[The identity]
$\mathcal{L}(\boldsymbol\theta) = \dfrac{N}{2\sigma^2}R_{\text{emp}}(\boldsymbol\theta) + \text{const}$ —
an **affine** function of the empirical risk, so identical minimisers. Measured agreement: $1.8\times10^{-15}$.

**Least squares is a consequence of Gaussian noise.** Laplace noise gives the **median** instead:
measured $3.420000$ against $1.400000$ on the same five observations.
:::

**Asymptotics (§8.3.2's remark).** The MLE is consistent, asymptotically normal, and its error variance
decays as $1/N$ — but *"the size of the samples necessary to achieve these properties can be quite
large"*, and *"especially in the 'small' data regime, maximum likelihood estimation can lead to
overfitting."*

### §8.3.2 Maximum a posteriori

| what | equation | notes |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Bayes for parameters | $p(\boldsymbol\theta \mid \mathbf{x}) = \dfrac{p(\mathbf{x} \mid \boldsymbol\theta)p(\boldsymbol\theta)}{p(\mathbf{x})}$ | **Eq 8.19** |
| the proportionality | $p(\boldsymbol\theta \mid \mathbf{x}) \propto p(\mathbf{x} \mid \boldsymbol\theta)p(\boldsymbol\theta)$ | **Eq 8.20** — $p(\mathbf{x})$ is constant in $\boldsymbol\theta$ |
| MAP objective | minimise $-\log p(\mathbf{x} \mid \boldsymbol\theta) - \log p(\boldsymbol\theta)$ | one extra term |
| **the correspondence** | $\boxed{\lambda = \dfrac{\sigma^2}{N\tau^2}}$ for $p(\boldsymbol\theta) = \mathcal{N}(\mathbf{0}, \tau^2\mathbf{I})$ | **ridge and MAP are the same estimator** |
| inverted | $\tau^2 = \dfrac{\sigma^2}{N\lambda}$ | to state a tuned $\lambda$ as a prior |
| Gaussian mean, closed form | $\hat\theta_{\text{MAP}} = \dfrac{N}{N + \sigma^2/\tau^2}\bar{y} = \dfrac{\bar{y}}{1+\lambda}$ | shrinkage toward zero |

Measured across $\tau^2 \in [10^{-2}, 10^{6}]$: worst $\lvert\text{ridge} - \text{MAP}\rvert = 1.24\times10^{-14}$.

| prior | implied penalty | behaviour |
| --- | --- | --- |
| $\mathcal{N}(\mathbf{0}, \tau^2\mathbf{I})$ | $\lambda\lVert\boldsymbol\theta\rVert^2$, ridge | smooth shrinkage |
| Laplace | $\lambda\lVert\boldsymbol\theta\rVert_1$, lasso | exact zeros |
| uniform (improper) | none | recovers the MLE |
| $\mathcal{N}(\mathbf{m}, \tau^2\mathbf{I})$ | $\lambda\lVert\boldsymbol\theta - \mathbf{m}\rVert^2$ | shrinks toward $\mathbf{m}$ |

:::caution[An isotropic prior shrinks the norm, not each coefficient]
Measured on Table 8.2: the intercept falls monotonically from $8.8074$ while the slope **rises** from
$2.1225$ to a peak of $2.2988$ at $\tau^2 = 31.71$ before falling. Centre your inputs and exclude the
intercept from the penalty.
:::

| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| overfitting | $11$ | $\mathbf{0.005232}$ | $3.9173$ | $748.8$ | $3616.98$ |
| underfitting | $1$ | $0.477771$ | $0.7254$ | $1.5$ | $0.81$ |
| fitting well | $4$ | $0.113967$ | $\mathbf{0.3806}$ | $3.3$ | $5.67$ |

**Overfitting has the lowest training risk of the three.** Underfitting is visible from training data
alone; overfitting is not — the parameter norm is the only training-side warning.

**The risk decomposition**, verified to Monte Carlo error:

$$
\mathbb{E}[\text{risk}] = \underbrace{\sigma^2}_{\text{noise}} + \underbrace{\mathbb{E}_x\!\left[(\bar{f}(x) - f^*(x))^2\right]}_{\text{bias}^2} + \underbrace{\mathbb{E}_x\!\left[\mathrm{Var}(f(x))\right]}_{\text{variance}}
$$

### §8.3.4 Generalized linear models

$$
p(y_n \mid \mathbf{x}_n, \boldsymbol\theta) = \phi(\boldsymbol\theta^\top\mathbf{x}_n) \qquad \text{(Eq 8.21)}
$$

$\phi$ is the **link function**; the family is the **generalized linear model** (McCullagh and Nelder,
1989; Agresti, 2002). Binary classification and count data live here, via the exponential family of §6.6.

## §8.4 Probabilistic modeling and inference

**§8.4.1's definition:** *a probabilistic model is specified by the joint distribution of all its random
variables.* Everything else follows by a rule of probability:

| what you want | from the joint, by |
| --- | --- |
| prior and likelihood | the **product rule** — they are its factors |
| marginal likelihood $p(\mathbf{x})$ | the **sum rule** — integrate out $\boldsymbol\theta$ |
| posterior | divide the joint by the marginal |

### §8.4.2 Bayesian inference

| what | equation |
| --- | --- |
| **posterior** | $p(\boldsymbol\theta \mid \mathcal{X}) = \dfrac{p(\mathcal{X} \mid \boldsymbol\theta)p(\boldsymbol\theta)}{p(\mathcal{X})}$, $\ p(\mathcal{X}) = \displaystyle\int p(\mathcal{X} \mid \boldsymbol\theta)p(\boldsymbol\theta)\,d\boldsymbol\theta$ — **Eq 8.22** |
| **predictive** | $p(\mathbf{x}) = \displaystyle\int p(\mathbf{x} \mid \boldsymbol\theta)p(\boldsymbol\theta)\,d\boldsymbol\theta = \mathbb{E}_{\boldsymbol\theta}[p(\mathbf{x} \mid \boldsymbol\theta)]$ — **Eq 8.23** |

**Gaussian closed form.** With $\boldsymbol\Sigma_N = \left(\boldsymbol\Phi^\top\boldsymbol\Phi/\sigma^2 + \mathbf{I}/\tau^2\right)^{-1}$
and $\mathbf{m}_N = \boldsymbol\Sigma_N\boldsymbol\Phi^\top\mathbf{y}/\sigma^2$:

$$
\text{mean} = \boldsymbol\phi^\top\mathbf{m}_N, \qquad \text{variance} = \underbrace{\sigma^2}_{\text{noise}} + \underbrace{\boldsymbol\phi^\top\boldsymbol\Sigma_N\boldsymbol\phi}_{\text{not knowing }\boldsymbol\theta}
$$

The mean is **identical** to the plug-in prediction. $\mathbf{m}_N$ equals the MAP estimate to
$8.88\times10^{-16}$.

Scalar case: $\dfrac{1}{\sigma_N^2} = \dfrac{N}{\sigma^2} + \dfrac{1}{\tau^2}$ — **precisions add**.

:::tip[Coverage of a nominal 95% interval, 4000 trials]
| region | plug-in $p(x \mid \boldsymbol\theta^*)$ | Eq 8.23 | plug width | full width |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| interpolation | $0.9287$ | $\mathbf{0.9506}$ | $1.3720$ | $1.4903$ |
| mild extrapolation | $0.5701$ | $\mathbf{0.9501}$ | $1.3720$ | $3.8869$ |
| far extrapolation | $\mathbf{0.1796}$ | $\mathbf{0.9498}$ | $1.3720$ | $13.2538$ |

The plug-in width is $2 \times 1.96 \times \sigma = 1.3720$ **everywhere**, because it does not depend
on $x$.
:::

| $N$ | largest posterior sd | full / plug-in width |
| --- | --- | --- |
| $5$ | $0.821958$ | $1.248213$ |
| $25$ | $0.392692$ | $1.039472$ |
| $1000$ | $0.074293$ | $1.001109$ |
| $10000$ | $0.022878$ | $\mathbf{1.000113}$ |

**Failure modes, measured.** Misspecified model class (sine data, cubic model): full predictive reaches
only $0.8908$. Misspecified noise scale (assumed $\sigma$ half the truth): coverage falls to $0.6697$.

**Approximations** when the prior is not conjugate: MCMC; the Laplace approximation; variational
inference; expectation propagation.

### §8.4.3 Latent-variable models

| what | equation |
| --- | --- |
| the generative conditional | $p(\mathbf{x} \mid \boldsymbol\theta, \mathbf{z})$ — **Eq 8.24** |
| **the likelihood** | $p(\mathbf{x} \mid \boldsymbol\theta) = \displaystyle\int p(\mathbf{x} \mid \boldsymbol\theta, \mathbf{z})p(\mathbf{z})\,d\mathbf{z}$ — **Eq 8.25** |
| parameter posterior | $p(\boldsymbol\theta \mid \mathcal{X}) = p(\mathcal{X} \mid \boldsymbol\theta)p(\boldsymbol\theta)/p(\mathcal{X})$ — **Eq 8.26** |
| latent posterior | $p(\mathbf{z} \mid \mathcal{X}) = p(\mathcal{X} \mid \mathbf{z})p(\mathbf{z})/p(\mathcal{X})$ — **Eq 8.27** |
| **the tractable one** | $p(\mathbf{z} \mid \mathcal{X}, \boldsymbol\theta) = \dfrac{p(\mathcal{X} \mid \mathbf{z}, \boldsymbol\theta)p(\mathbf{z})}{p(\mathcal{X} \mid \boldsymbol\theta)}$ — **Eq 8.28** |

**Equation 8.25's rule:** the likelihood *must not* depend on $\mathbf{z}$ — only on $\mathbf{x}$ and
$\boldsymbol\theta$. Marginalising both $\mathbf{z}$ and $\boldsymbol\theta$ at once is *"not possible in
general"*; Eq 8.28 is the compromise, and it is what Chapters 10 and 11 actually compute.

**EM**, measured on a two-component mixture: the E step is Eq 8.28, the M step maximises given it, and the
likelihood never falls — smallest step over 40 iterations $-9.09\times10^{-13}$, floating-point noise at a
converged optimum. No step size, no line search. **No guarantee about which local maximum.**

## §8.5 Directed graphical models

| what | rule |
| --- | --- |
| nodes | random variables |
| arrows | conditional probabilities; $a \to b$ carries $p(b \mid a)$ |
| shaded node | **observed**; unshaded is latent |
| plate | repeat everything inside $N$ times |
| hyperprior | a prior on a prior's parameters; deterministic parameters lose their circle |
| **factorization → graph** | one node per variable; an arrow in from every variable it is conditioned on |
| **graph → factorization** | **Eq 8.31**: $p(\mathbf{x}) = \prod_{k=1}^{K} p(x_k \mid \mathrm{Pa}_k)$ |

| $K$ binary variables | full joint, $2^K-1$ | $\le 2$ parents each | ratio |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| $5$ | $31$ | $15$ | $2.067$ |
| $20$ | $1.049\times10^{6}$ | $75$ | $1.398\times10^{4}$ |
| $100$ | $\mathbf{1.268\times10^{30}}$ | $\mathbf{395}$ | $3.209\times10^{27}$ |

:::caution[The layout depends on the factorization, not the joint]
$p(a)p(b \mid a)$ reconstructs a joint to $0.00\times10^{0}$ and $p(b)p(a \mid b)$ to $5.55\times10^{-17}$
— two correct graphs pointing opposite ways. An arrow means causation only *"with additional
assumptions (Pearl, 2009)"*.
:::

### d-separation — Eq 8.34

$A \perp\!\!\!\perp B \mid C$ iff **every trail** (path ignoring arrow direction) from $A$ to $B$ is blocked.

| meeting | shape | blocked when | conditioning… |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| head to tail | $u \to n \to v$ | $n \in C$ | **blocks** |
| tail to tail | $u \leftarrow n \to v$ | $n \in C$ | **blocks** |
| **head to head** | $u \to n \leftarrow v$ | $n \notin C$ **and** no descendant of $n$ in $C$ | **opens** |

Example 8.9 on Figure 8.11 ($a \to b$, $a \to d$, $b \to c$, $c \to d$, $d \to e$), measured on
$4{,}000{,}000$ samples:

| eq | claim | book says | partial correlation |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 8.35 | $b \perp\!\!\!\perp d \mid a, c$ | independent | $-0.000320$ |
| 8.36 | $a \perp\!\!\!\perp c \mid b$ | independent | $-0.000079$ |
| 8.37 | $b \not\perp\!\!\!\perp d \mid c$ | dependent | $0.272172$ |
| 8.38 | $a \not\perp\!\!\!\perp c \mid b, e$ | dependent | $-0.146064$ |

**The collider, isolated:** $\mathrm{corr}(a,c)$ is $-0.000079$ given $\{b\}$, $-0.237939$ given
$\{b, d\}$, $-0.146064$ given $\{b, e\}$. Conditioning on a **descendant** is enough.

**Three families (§8.5.3):** directed (Bayesian networks), undirected (Markov random fields), factor
graphs. *"Not every distribution can be represented in a particular choice of graphical model."*

## §8.6 Model selection

### §8.6.1 Nested cross-validation

**Inner loop chooses** (the validation set); **outer loop reports** (the test set).

:::danger[Why, measured]
$600$ trials, $60$ candidate features with **no signal at all**, $n = 30$:

| | value |
| --- | --- |
| flat CV score of the winner | $0.865343$ |
| its true risk on fresh data | $1.254179$ |
| chance | $1.000000$ |

Flat CV reports $13.5\%$ **better** than chance on data containing nothing. The bias tracks **how hard
you searched** — with only $10$ candidate degrees on $60$ points it is about $0.5\%$.
:::

### §8.6.2 Bayesian model selection

| what | equation |
| --- | --- |
| generative process | $M_k \sim p(M)$, $\ \boldsymbol\theta_k \sim p(\boldsymbol\theta \mid M_k)$, $\ \mathcal{D} \sim p(\mathcal{D} \mid \boldsymbol\theta_k)$ — **Eq 8.40–8.42** |
| **model posterior** | $p(M_k \mid \mathcal{D}) \propto p(M_k)\,p(\mathcal{D} \mid M_k)$ — **Eq 8.43** |
| **model evidence** | $p(\mathcal{D} \mid M_k) = \displaystyle\int p(\mathcal{D} \mid \boldsymbol\theta_k)p(\boldsymbol\theta_k \mid M_k)\,d\boldsymbol\theta_k$ — **Eq 8.44** |
| the choice | $M^* = \arg\max_{M_k} p(M_k \mid \mathcal{D})$ — **Eq 8.45** |

With a uniform $p(M_k) = 1/K$, Eq 8.45 reduces to **maximising the evidence**.

**The automatic Occam's razor.** No prior favouring simple models is needed: $p(\mathcal{D} \mid M)$ is a
normalized distribution **over datasets**, so breadth costs depth. Measured over $200{,}000$ datasets:

$$
\mathrm{KL}(M_1 \Vert M_2) = 3.4062 \text{ nats}, \qquad \mathrm{KL}(M_2 \Vert M_1) = 50.7124 \text{ nats}
$$

Both positive necessarily — each model beats the other on its own data, so **none can win everywhere**.
That is Figure 8.14. Region $C$'s edge, measured: $w = 0.571273$.

**The evidence splits exactly** (verified to $5.68\times10^{-14}$), with
$\mathbf{A} = \boldsymbol\Phi^\top\boldsymbol\Phi/\sigma^2 + \mathbf{I}/\tau^2$:

$$
\log p(\mathcal{D} \mid M) = \underbrace{\log p(\mathcal{D} \mid \mathbf{m})}_{\text{best fit}} \underbrace{- \frac{\lVert\mathbf{m}\rVert^2}{2\tau^2} - \frac{D}{2}\log\tau^2 - \frac{1}{2}\log\lvert\mathbf{A}\rvert}_{\text{Occam factor}}
$$

Degree $1 \to 11$: fit improves by $45.70$, Occam factor worsens by $12.67$. **The penalty is real,
automatic, and gentle.**

### §8.6.3 Bayes factors

$$
\underbrace{\frac{p(M_1 \mid \mathcal{D})}{p(M_2 \mid \mathcal{D})}}_{\text{posterior odds}} = \underbrace{\frac{p(M_1)}{p(M_2)}}_{\text{prior odds}}\ \underbrace{\frac{p(\mathcal{D} \mid M_1)}{p(\mathcal{D} \mid M_2)}}_{\text{Bayes factor}} \qquad \text{(Eq 8.46)}
$$

With a uniform model prior this reduces to the Bayes factor alone (**Eq 8.47**): above $1$ choose $M_1$,
below it $M_2$.

| log Bayes factor | Jeffreys (1961) |
| --- | --- |
| $< \log 3$ | barely worth mentioning |
| $\log 3$ to $\log 10$ | substantial |
| $\log 10$ to $\log 30$ | strong |
| $\log 30$ to $\log 100$ | very strong |
| $> \log 100$ | decisive |

Measured on 25 points: degrees $0$–$2$ are **decisive** ($70.83$, $33.28$, $31.50$); degrees $3$–$11$
are **all** *"barely worth mentioning"*. The evidence detects underfitting sharply and overfitting
bluntly.

:::danger[The Jeffreys–Lindley paradox]
*"The Bayes factor always favors the simpler model since the probability of the data under a complex
model with a diffuse prior will be very small"* (Murphy, 2012).

With the **data held fixed**, sweeping only $\tau^2$: the log Bayes factor (degree 1 over degree 9)
bottoms out at $-39.4634$ at $\tau^2 = 10.22$, crosses zero at $\tau^2 = \mathbf{3.71412\times10^{6}}$,
and reaches $+46.1873$ at $10^{14}$ — still climbing.

**A Bayes factor quoted without its prior is not a reproducible number.**
:::

### §8.6.4 Information criteria

| criterion | formula | equation |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **AIC** | $\log p(\mathbf{x} \mid \boldsymbol\theta) - M$ | **Eq 8.48** |
| **BIC** | $\log p(\mathbf{x} \mid \boldsymbol\theta) - \tfrac{1}{2}M\log N$ | **Eq 8.49** |

Choose the **largest** value. BIC's penalty exceeds AIC's whenever $N > e^2 \approx 7.39$, and the ratio
is $\tfrac{1}{2}\log N$.

| criterion | picks, on the chapter's 25 points |
| --- | --- |
| maximum likelihood | degree $\mathbf{11}$ — the largest on offer, always |
| AIC | degree $5$ |
| BIC | degree $5$ |
| exact evidence | degree $6$ |
| cross-validation | degree $8$, with three statistical ties |

**BIC as an approximation:** mean absolute error $8.0274$ nats, worst $13.4474$, at $N = 25$. It is a
large-$N$ approximation. The **ranking** survives; the **value** does not. Its derivation drops the prior
entirely — which is both why it is inaccurate and why it is immune to the Jeffreys–Lindley paradox.

## The chapter in ten lines

1. **Empirical risk is computable; expected risk is what you want.** Everything else follows from that gap.
2. **A penalty and a prior are the same object**, related by $\lambda = \sigma^2/(N\tau^2)$, measured to $1.24\times10^{-14}$.
3. **Least squares is a consequence of Gaussian noise**, not a definition of fitting.
4. **Overfitting has the lowest training risk.** It is invisible without held-out data or a look at $\lVert\boldsymbol\theta\rVert$.
5. **A point estimate is not wrong, it is incomplete.** The posterior mean equals the MAP point; the covariance is what was discarded.
6. **Equation 8.23 is calibrated where a plug-in interval is not** — $0.9498$ against $0.1796$.
7. **Estimation is optimization; inference is integration.** They converge as $N$ grows, ratio $1.000113$ at $N = 10000$.
8. **A graph is a factorization**, and d-separation reads independence off it — four for four on Example 8.9.
9. **Conditioning on a collider opens a path.** It is the only rule that runs backwards.
10. **The Occam's razor is automatic and gentle**, and every model-selection number is hostage to a prior or to how hard you searched.
