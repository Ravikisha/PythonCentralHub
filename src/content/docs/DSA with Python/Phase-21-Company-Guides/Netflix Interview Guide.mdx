Netflix runs the most **senior-weighted** loop here. It hires few people, at high levels, and the
interview reflects that: less algorithmic puzzle-solving, far more on judgement, autonomy, and
whether you can operate without process around you. The published culture memo is not marketing --
it is close to a rubric, and reading it is genuine preparation.

Expect practical, systems-flavoured problems over contest-style algorithms, and expect to justify
decisions rather than derive optimal complexities. **This page's problem list is derived from pattern
families rather than reports** -- there is very little reliable crowd data on Netflix.

:::caution[Crowd-reported, not published]
No company publishes its question bank. The round structure and the bar below are corroborated by recruiters and candidates; the **problem list is crowd-reported, stale, and biased toward whoever posted it**. Treat the *pattern families* as the durable signal -- which families a company leans on changes far more slowly than which individual problems it uses.
:::

## Pitfalls

- **Preparing like it is a LeetCode loop.** The algorithmic content is real but secondary. Judgement, ownership, and communication carry more weight.
- **Not reading the culture memo.** It functions as a rubric for the behavioural conversation, and candidates who have engaged with it are visibly better prepared.
- **Answering as an implementer rather than an owner.** "I was told to build X" is a weak answer at this level. What did you decide, and what did you decline to do?
- **Expecting a junior-friendly bar.** Few hires, high levels. If your experience does not yet include owning ambiguous work end to end, this is the hardest loop on this page.

## Interview follow-ups

| They ask | What they're checking | The answer |
| --- | --- | --- |
| "What did you decide not to do?" | Judgement and prioritisation | A real trade-off you made, the thing you deliberately dropped, and why. Senior signal is what you *declined*, not just what you shipped. |
| "How did you operate without a process?" | Autonomy | A concrete case where you set the direction. Netflix screens for people who do not need scaffolding. |
| "Would you push back on this requirement?" | Candour, per the culture memo | Yes, with the reasoning and the data -- and then say what you would do if overruled. Disagreement is expected here; disagreement without a follow-through plan is not. |
| "Design something practical" (rather than a puzzle) | Systems judgement | Talk trade-offs, failure modes and operational cost rather than optimal asymptotics. This is the shape the round usually takes. |

## Recall card

- **Most senior-weighted loop here.** Few hires, high levels, judgement over puzzles.
- **Read the culture memo.** It is close to a rubric for the behavioural conversation.
- **Answer as an owner, not an implementer** -- what you decided, and what you declined.
- **Practical and systems-flavoured problems** over contest algorithms. Trade-offs beat asymptotics.
- **Least reliable crowd data of any company here** -- the list below is pattern-derived, so weight the families.
